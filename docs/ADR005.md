# ADR005: The random term has memory

**Status.** Decided by Aditya on 4 Oct 2026 and agreed by Suné on 6 Oct, since the change is
in her modules. **Calibrated and confirmed in the engine on 6 Oct (§3): σ_u = 0.226 m/s, about
90 % coverage at every horizon from 1 to 24 h.**
**Date.** 2026-10-06.
**Amends.** D009's step (`docs/position-update.md`) and D028's σ (`docs/sigma-calibration.md`).
**Does not change** D002: the model is still three terms, $v_d = v_c + \alpha v_{w10} + \eta(t)$.
Only $\eta$ gains memory.
**Project record.** Vault `decisions/D030 The random term has memory.md` holds the framing
and the citations. This is the engineering contract.

## Question

The engine's random term was a random walk: every minute, each particle got a fresh push of
$\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}$ in a random direction. So the cloud spread as $\sqrt t$, and one σ
could be right at one horizon only (vault L22).

The search episode (#47, ADR004 §4) showed that the random walk also changes *which search
wins*. Swept lanes fill back in, because every particle jumps about 204 m a minute.

**Is the drift error a random walk, or a velocity error that persists? And what does the
engine do about it?**

## Decision

| Row | Decision | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **The term** | Each particle carries a velocity error $u$ that forgets itself over $T_L$: a first-order Markov ("random flight") process. Each step moves the particle by $(v_d + u)\,\Delta t$, then updates $u \leftarrow a\,u + \sqrt{1-a^2}\,\sigma_u Z$, with $a = e^{-\Delta t/T_L}$ | Taylor (1921) gives its spread; Griffa (1996) names it; Ullman et al. (2006) used it for US Coast Guard search and rescue and found the random walk "inconsistent with observations" (§1) |
| **The start** | $u_0 \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_u^2)$ per axis, the process's steady state | Released from one point (D026), the cloud then spreads in a straight line from the first step, as the drifters do. A start at zero grows too slowly through the first hours, which are the search's |
| **The update** | Exact for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, so $\mathrm{var}(u) = \sigma_u^2$ at any step | At 60 s and $T_L$ = 25.7 h, $a$ = 0.99935 and the fresh part is $0.036\,\sigma_u$ |
| **$T_L$** | **25.7 h**, fixed. `MEMORY_TIME_S` in `sar.model.position` | Fitted to the drifters' spread at 1–48 h (§1). About one inertial period at 26.5 N (26.8 h) |
| **$\sigma_u$** | Matched in the engine at 4 h, as σ was (D028), plus the person's crosswind slide as a velocity. `CALIBRATED_SIGMA_U` | §3 |
| **One term at a time** | `DriftPipeline(sigma=...)` is the random walk and `DriftPipeline(sigma_u=..., memory_time_s=...)` the random velocity. Asking for both is refused. Neither is the deterministic path | No silent mixture of two calibrations |
| **Where the state lives** | `track` carries $u$ from step to step and passes it to `advance(positions, time, velocity_error)`. `TrackState.velocity_error` reports it | `advance` stays a function of its arguments, so `calibrate_sigma` and `sar.search.datum`, which call it directly, cannot inherit a hidden state |
| **The default** | `sar.pipeline.ensemble`'s CLI defaults to the random velocity. `--sigma` still asks for the random walk, for comparisons with earlier runs. The library functions keep their deterministic defaults | As #94 did for σ: the default changes in the PR that carries the calibrated value |
| **The old term** | Unchanged bit for bit. Two tests pin outputs recorded on `main` before the change | Runs and calibrations made with the random walk stay reproducible |

## Evidence

### 1 · The drifters say "velocity with memory"

`scripts/fit_random_velocity.py` (PR #96) reads the σ calibration's own outputs
(`fit.json` at 4 h and 24 h, and `lagcheck.json`). Taylor's spread,

$$s(t)^2 = 2\sigma_u^2 T_L\left[t - T_L\left(1 - e^{-t/T_L}\right)\right],$$

fits the undrogued dev drifters' 90 % spread at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12, 24 and 48 h with
**$\sigma_u$ = 0.217 m/s and $T_L$ = 25.7 h**, an rms log error of **0.018 (about 2 %)**. The
best single random-walk σ is off by **0.526 (about 50 %)**.

| Horizon | 1 h | 4 h | 24 h | 48 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drifters, 90 % spread per axis | 0.78 km | 3.07 km | 15.79 km | 29.43 km |
| Random velocity | 0.78 | 3.05 | 16.25 | 28.74 |
| Random walk, σ = 26.3 | 1.58 | 3.16 | 7.73 | 10.93 |

**An independent check.** HYCOM's current at each start against the buoy's own velocity
differs by **0.228 m/s RMS per axis** (2,807 windows, job 58773). The fit's 0.217 is within
5 %. The random term is HYCOM's velocity error, held.

### 2 · What it does to a search

Clouds released from one point, arrival 2 h after the call, Expanding Square against Sector
Search (`fit_random_velocity.py --search`):

| Model | Spread at arrival | Expanding Square POS | Sector Search POS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random walk | 2.23 km | 0.309 | 0.331 |
| **Random velocity** | 1.54 km | **0.569** | 0.368 |

### 3 · Calibrated and confirmed in the engine

T_L fixed at 25.7 h, σ_u laddered at 0.12–0.32 m/s on the 12,973 dev windows, 1,000
particles each (array 58974). σ_u* = 0.223 (0.215–0.233), the crosswind slide added as
0.035 m/s, so **σ_u = 0.226 m/s (0.217–0.236)**. Confirmed at exactly 0.226 (array 58987),
inside the 90 % region:

| | 1 h | 2 h | 3 h | 4 h | 6 h | 12 h | 24 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Undrogued, random velocity | **90.2 %** | 90.4 | 90.6 | **90.5** | 90.8 | 91.4 | **91.7 %** |
| Undrogued, random walk (σ = 26.3) | 98.8 % | 96.8 | 93.8 | 90.3 | 83.2 | 68.1 | 51.7 % |
| Median 90 % area, random velocity | 9 km² | 37 | 83 | 146 | 319 | 1,186 | 4,134 |

**The test D030 set, about 90 % at every horizon, is passed.** The random walk can only pass
it at the one horizon it is matched to. Details and intervals: `docs/sigma-calibration.md`
§00.

## Consequences

- **`calibrate_sigma ladder --sigmas-u`** runs the same ladder over $\sigma_u$ with $T_L$
  fixed. `calibrate` reads the model from the rows and adds the slide as a velocity,
  $\sigma_c/\sqrt T$.
- **Every cloud made before this is a random walk.** That includes any scenario run.
  Regenerate them.
- **D024's particle ladder reruns.** The cloud is narrower.
- **The episode (#47, ADR004) is unchanged.** Its straight line within a step is now
  physically right, not only right for the engine.
- **CLAUDE.md's frozen-model line** gains "η has memory".

## Not decided here

- A $T_L$ per water type (the jet against quiet water). The pooled value comes first.
- Each particle's crosswind slide as a fixed side, as Breivik & Allen (2008) do. Here it is
  folded into $\sigma_u$'s variance, as D028 folded it into σ.
- The inertial swing. The HYCOM–buoy agreement dips at ±12 h (`lagcheck.json`), which is
  oscillatory. An exponential memory is the simplest model that fits, not the exact one.
