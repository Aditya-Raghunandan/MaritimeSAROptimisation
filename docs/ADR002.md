# ADR002: Probability map interface

**Status.** Proposed. Eleven rows are decided on the engine side; six questions marked **†**
require agreement from the search half before the contract is frozen.
**Date.** 2026-09-23 (the deadline was 21 Sep).
**Supersedes.** Nothing. **Blocks.** Issue #7 (Grid cell translation), `src/sar/model/probmap.py`,
`src/sar/model/drift.py`, and the RL environment.
**Project record.** The vault decision `decisions/D007 Probability map interface.md` carries the
project-level framing, the report-facing citation gaps and the schedule consequences. This document
is the engineering contract: what is built, against what numbers, and how it is tested.

## Question

The probability map is the single hand-off between the two halves of the project. One half's
simulator produces it; the other half's search layer consumes it, and **the map is the agent's
observation**, so the reinforcement-learning environment cannot be built until its shape is fixed.

Issue #7 requests `position_to_cell` and `cell_to_position` with a single argument
`resolution=GRID_RESOLUTION_DEG`, defaulting to **1/12°, square, "matching the HYCOM native
grid"**. That specification cannot be implemented as written, for three reasons.

1. **The HYCOM grid used here is neither 1/12° nor square.** Measured from a file produced by this
   repository: 476 latitude × 238 longitude points at **0.04° latitude × 0.08° longitude**, which
   is 4.45 km north–south against **7.97 km** east–west at 26.5 N. Latitude is the finer axis. A
   single scalar `resolution` cannot express an anisotropic grid at all.
2. **All four of the issue's acceptance criteria would pass against a square grid that does not
   exist.** The defect would surface much later, as a north–south bias in the binned Monte Carlo
   output, and would be indistinguishable from a physical result.
3. **The probability map should not be on the forcing grid.** An 8 km cell is approximately 43
   times the sweep width, which is the length scale the map exists to resolve.

The question is therefore not "what resolution", but **which grid is authoritative, and what the
conversions between grids are.**

## Decision

### The word *grid* has been denoting three distinct objects

| # | Grid | Cell size | Determined by | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Forcing** | ERA5 0.25° (≈28 km); HYCOM 0.08° lon × 0.04° lat (≈8 × 4.5 km) | the data products; given, not chosen | — |
| 2 | **Probability map** | **250–500 m** | sweep width *W* ≈ 185 m. Cells much larger than *W* cannot distinguish "just searched" from "one kilometre to the side", which blunts the reward; cells much smaller are wasted, because detection is not resolved below *W* | drift half (engine emits) |
| 3 | **Agent observation** | 32×32 to 64×64 over the box | what a convolutional policy under PPO can learn from | search half (env consumes) |

These are related by documented conversions, not by being the same array. Because they are three
objects, **each cell size is a parameter in the code and not a module-level constant**: the best
observation grid is not known and should not be asserted, and the training runs can determine it.

### The contract

Rows marked **†** require agreement from the search half.

| Row | Decision | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Coordinate convention** | latitude/longitude in degrees, with **separate `dlat` and `dlon`**, sized so that cells are square *in metres* at the box centre | preserves the existing `{lat0, dlat, nlat, lon0, dlon, nlon}` contract shared by `sar.viz.export.grid_spec` and `frontend/src/layers.js`. No projection is introduced. Evidence §1 |
| **Extent †** | **one fixed box per scenario, identical in size across scenarios, anchored on the particle cloud at the searcher's arrival time.** Approximately 100 km | a box pinned at the datum is empty within hours; a box sized to contain an entire run is 495 km at 48 h, and the study domain is 2100 × 1900 km. A constant box size means one agent cell always denotes the same distance. Mass outside the box is logged as `lost_mass`; the boundary is a wall for the agent. Evidence §3 |
| **Cell size** | **a required parameter with no default.** Working values: 250 m for the physics map, 500 m for the archive | of order *W*. The default in issue #7 is the defect |
| **Density estimate** | **plain binning at N = 10⁶ particles.** Kernel density estimation is rejected | KDE inflates the estimated spread and would corrupt validation target R2c. Evidence §2. The particle count is affordable: Evidence §5 |
| **Coverage and detection †** | **swept on the particles, not on a grid mask.** At each step, any particle within *W*/2 of the track segment has its weight reduced (to zero, or by a factor 1 − POD). Each map is then a rendering of the remaining weight | this is what requirement R4c already specifies. Three properties follow without further work: the reward is exact however coarse the agent's observation is; coverage advects with the water automatically; and the baseline pattern and the trained policy are scored by identical code. Evidence §4 |
| **Agent observation †** | **two channels** — A: the whole box, coarse (32×32); B: a fine window centred on the searcher (approximately 32×32 at 250 m, ≈8 km across) so that its own tracks are visible. Both sizes remain parameters | at 64×64 over 100 km one agent cell is 1562 m, and a single pass sweeps 185 m of it, leaving **88.2 %** of the cell unsearched |
| **Coarsening** | a named and tested `coarsen(n)` that is **mass-preserving** — sum, then renormalise — rather than an image resize | R5a requires a coarsened observation; this must be a documented function, not an ad-hoc interpolation |
| **Time axis** | **two rates**: 15 min from datum to arrival, and **60 s** through the 45-minute on-scene window. Arrival time is a scenario input, not an agent choice | 15 min corresponds to 1.62 km of drift, or 6.5 cells at 250 m; 60 s corresponds to 108 m, under one sweep width. R7a fixes the transit and D012 places the window in daylight, so the coarse-then-fine axis is precomputable |
| **Normalisation** | per timestep, summing to 1, with beached mass retained | decided previously in the vault at D016. **The reward is accumulated on the raw weight before renormalisation**, or rewards are not comparable between timesteps |
| **Mass leaving the grid** | beached particles are frozen in place and retain their mass; `beached_mass` and `lost_mass` are logged per timestep | D016 |
| **File format** | NetCDF/Zarr with CF conventions | D009 |

Two consequences of the time axis had not previously been written down. The slice cadence **is**
the agent's decision interval, so a 45-minute window at 60 s makes the episode **exactly 45 steps**.
And D009's ordering obliges `probmap.py` **before** `drift.py`, because the output format is the
contract the search half is blocked on, and it can be specified, validated and agreed before any
physics exists.

## Evidence

All quantities below derive from five inputs: *W* = 185 m, a representative Gulf Stream speed of
1.8 m/s, a box centre at 26.5 N, 111.32 km per degree of latitude, and a 45-minute on-scene window.

### 1 · Square cells without a map projection

Δlat = 250 m ÷ 111.32 km = **0.002246°**; Δlon = Δlat ÷ cos(26.5°) = **0.002509°**; the ratio is
**1.117**. Across a 100 km box the residual distortion is **0.78 %**, or about one metre on a 250 m
cell. Across the full 17–36 N domain the same effect is **15.3 %**, which is a second and
independent reason the map must never span the domain. That ratio is precisely what a single
`resolution` argument cannot express.

### 2 · Why kernel density estimation is rejected

Smoothing the particle cloud with a bandwidth *h* is the conventional remedy for a speckled map. It
is rejected here for three reasons, the third of which is decisive.

- The drift model already carries a stochastic term and a perturbed datum; the ensemble spread
  **is** the uncertainty estimate. A bandwidth adds a second, non-physical smoothing on top of it.
- It introduces a further hyperparameter to optimise.
- **It would corrupt validation target R2c.** R2c validates the drift model by the fraction of true
  drifter positions falling inside the **90 % probability contour**. A Gaussian KDE inflates the
  estimated spread from σ to √(σ² + h²), so the contour is systematically too large and more true
  positions fall inside it than should. **The validation would pass more easily as a consequence of
  an estimator choice.** At *h* = 1 km:

  | true ensemble spread σ | contour inflation |
  |---|---|
  | 2 km (early, tight) | **+11.8 %** |
  | 5 km | +2.0 % |
  | 10 km | +0.5 % |
  | 20 km | +0.1 % |

  The distortion is largest exactly when the ensemble is tight, which is when the search begins and
  when the map carries most information.

The speckle that KDE would conceal is nonetheless real. Binning into 250 m cells over a 100 km box,
assuming the drift corridor occupies about 15 % of it, the mean occupied cell holds 0.4 particles at
N = 10⁴ (**155 %** Poisson noise) and 4.2 particles at N = 10⁵ (**49 %**). The remedy is particles,
not blur.

### 3 · The box cannot be fixed at the datum

At 1.8 m/s a target travels **467 km in 72 h**, so a 100 km box pinned at the last known position is
empty within hours. The fine map, however, only needs to exist while a search is in progress, and
during the 45-minute on-scene window the cloud drifts **4.9 km** — so a box anchored at arrival is
stable for an entire episode. A box large enough to contain the whole run is not affordable:

| Box | Cells at 500 m | Per scenario (141 slices) | All 35 scenarios |
|---|---|---|---|
| **100 km (on-scene)** | 200 × 200 | **23 MB** | 0.8 GB |
| 400 km (48 h of travel) | 800 × 800 | 361 MB | 12.6 GB |
| 2100 km (the whole domain) | 4200 × 4200 | **9.9 GB** | 348 GB |

### 4 · Why coverage cannot be a grid mask

Two distinct problems arise. First, at 64×64 over a 100 km box an agent cell is 1562 m wide and a
single pass sweeps 185 m of it, leaving **88.2 %** of the cell unsearched. Second, and decisively, a
coverage mask pinned to the map becomes stale immediately, because the water moves **108 m per
minute** — the greater part of a sweep width. **A moving grid does not resolve the second problem
either.** Coverage must advect with the water, which means it must be recorded on the particles.

The same arithmetic expressed as a ribbon: one leg sweeps a 185 m ribbon, which is **0.74** of a
250 m cell and **0.37** of a 500 m cell. Flagging every cell a track crosses over-credits coverage by
up to **5.4×** at 1 km cells, and would systematically favour whichever strategy flies more track —
which is the comparison under test.

The cost of this decision is that the engine must deliver particle positions for the on-scene
window, not only rendered maps: approximately **370 MB per scenario**, or 13 GB across all 35. If
the sweep computation proves slow inside the training loop, the environment may subsample to 10⁵
particles.

### 5 · N = 10⁶ is affordable, measured rather than assumed

A stand-in Euler–Maruyama step (bilinear sample, update, noise) benchmarked in NumPy on 2026-09-22:

| N | ms per step | per 48 h scenario | all 35 on one core |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁴ | 0.8 | 2.2 s | 1.3 min |
| 10⁵ | 12.7 | 36.6 s | 21 min |
| **10⁶** | 175 | **504 s** | **294 min (≈5 h)** |

D009 already dispatches a Slurm array over scenarios, so 35 concurrent tasks reduce this to
approximately **8 minutes of wall-clock time**. D006's assertion that the Monte Carlo is not the
compute constraint — policy training is — survives the tenfold increase in particle count.

### 6 · Rendering the map costs nothing new on the client

| Layer | Grid | Bytes per frame | Relative to one HYCOM frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| HYCOM current (existing) | 476 × 238 | 453 kB | 1.0× |
| probability map, 500 m over 100 km | 200 × 200 | 160 kB | 0.4× |
| probability map, 250 m over 100 km | 400 × 400 | 640 kB | 1.4× |

`raster` is already one of the four declared `LAYER_TYPES`, and `RasterLayer.gridAt(frame)` already
reads a per-frame grid, because D021 forbids a renderer from assuming the grid. At 400 × 400 the
cell count sits between the existing wind raster (5,929) and the current raster (113,288). The
outstanding gap is on the Python side: **no writer exists for the plural `grids` form.**

### 7 · Curvature denotes three distinct effects, of which one matters

| Effect | Over a 100–200 km box | Across the 2100 km domain | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sphericity** (great-circle arc against planar chord) | 0.004 % | 0.45 % | **Ignored**, and stated as ignored. Below every other error in the system |
| **Meridian convergence** (the cos φ factor) | 0.78 % | **15.3 %** | **Handled** by separate `dlat` and `dlon`; also the reason the box must remain small |
| **Velocity conversion in the integrator** | — | **up to 18.2 %** | **Critical, and not a grid concern at all** |

The third effect belongs to `drift.py`. Converting an eastward velocity in m/s into a longitude rate
requires dividing by 111.32 km × cos φ. **If cos φ is evaluated once — at the datum, for instance —
and reused as the particle drifts north, every eastward step is wrong:**

| cos φ evaluated at | particle reaches | eastward speed in error by |
|---|---|---|
| 26.5 N | 30 N | 3.3 % |
| 26.5 N | 36 N | **10.6 %** |
| 17 N | 36 N | **18.2 %** |

A particle traversing the full latitude range of the Gulf Stream would be advected up to 18 % too
far east or west, **and the resulting trajectory would be indistinguishable from a physical one**.
No exception is raised and no diagnostic is affected.

This is the third defect of the same family found this month. The raster layer was painted 44 km
north of its true position because latitude was assumed linear on a Mercator projection (PR #36),
and a cell width was quoted as 8.07 km rather than 7.97 km because it was computed at the domain
edge rather than the box centre. In each case **a latitude-dependent factor was treated as a
constant.** It is a named failure mode for this project rather than three coincidences.

**Accordingly, cos φ is recomputed per particle at every step**, and a regression test pins it: a
particle advected due east at constant speed from 17 N and from 35 N must traverse *different*
longitude and the *same* distance in metres.

## The interface to be built

`src/sar/model/grid.py`, class `ProbabilityGrid`. Cell size is a **required argument with no
default**.

| Member | Contract |
|---|---|
| `from_envelope(lats, lons, cell_m, margin_km)` | fits the box to the particle spread **at arrival**, plus the searcher's reach; snaps to whole cells; derives `dlat` and `dlon` so that cells are square in metres at the box centre. It must never be fitted to the whole-run travel |
| `position_to_cell(lat, lon)` / `cell_to_position(j, i)` | the API requested in issue #7, satisfying all four of its acceptance criteria, but parameterised by `(dlat, dlon)` rather than by one `resolution` |
| `bin(lats, lons)` | particle positions to cell counts |
| `normalise(counts)` | sums to 1 per timestep, with beached mass included |
| `coarsen(n)` | mass-preserving; sum then renormalise |
| `to_spec()` | the existing six-field grid dict, so that `sar.viz.export` and `layers.js` consume it unchanged |
| `SWEEP_WIDTH_M` | **no Python copy of this constant currently exists**; `frontend/src/geo.js` holds the only one. It requires exactly one Python home and never a second |

Existing code to be reused rather than reimplemented: `sar.utils.geo.regular_axis_step` (the single
arbiter of axis regularity in this repository), `to_store_longitude` and `to_display_longitude`
(D020's boundary rule), `sar.model.interpolate.nearest_index`, `cell_corners` and
`bilinear_weights`, `sar.viz.export.grid_spec`, and on the client `layers.js::Grid` together with
`RasterLayer.gridAt(frame)`.

### Verification

Tests follow the existing convention: pure arithmetic, no network access.

- The four acceptance criteria from issue #7.
- `dlon / dlat = 1 / cos(lat)` at the box centre.
- `to_spec()` round-tripping through `grid_spec`'s own arithmetic.
- Normalisation summing to 1 **with beached mass included**.
- `coarsen` preserving total mass.
- **The equal-area test**, which is the one that would detect the latitude bias: translate an
  identical synthetic cloud from 17 N to 36 N and assert that total mass is invariant.
- **The cos φ test** described in Evidence §7.
- **Convergence (R1f):** run at N and 2N and compare centroid, spread and 90 % contour area. This
  converts "10⁶ particles were used" from an assumption into a result, and because the estimator is
  plain binning it is a clean test of N alone.
- **A noise measurement for the report:** at the chosen N and cell size, measure the actual
  distribution of particles per occupied cell rather than relying on the 15 % occupancy assumed in
  Evidence §2.
- **Visual inspection.** Render one scenario through the `raster` layer and run `npm run shots`.

## Consequences

- **Issue #7 cannot be implemented as specified.** Closing it requires a comment carrying the
  agreed API: two step sizes, a required resolution, and no HYCOM-matching default.
- **`probmap.py` precedes `drift.py`**, because the output format is the contract the search half is
  blocked on.
- **The engine acquires a third output product** — particle positions through the on-scene window,
  approximately 370 MB per scenario — in addition to the two tiers specified in D009.
- **`drift.py` inherits a hard requirement** it would not otherwise have had: cos φ recomputed per
  particle per step, with a test. It is not a grid concern, and was found only by asking what
  curvature means in this system.
- **A Python writer for the plural `grids` form** is now owed on the export side.
- **A limitation is registered:** beaching is detected from the HYCOM `_FillValue` land mask at
  approximately 8 km resolution, against a probability grid at 250–500 m. D016 already requires a
  GSHHG coastline cross-check "before any result depends on it", and this has not been performed.
- **If arrival time is later randomised** to improve generalisation, intermediate maps must be
  recomputed from stored particle positions and **never produced by linearly interpolating the
  15-minute slices**: in 15 minutes the distribution translates 6.5 cells, and a linear blend
  produces two ghost maxima rather than one displaced distribution. Recomputation is inexpensive,
  because the coverage decision already requires the particles to be retained.
