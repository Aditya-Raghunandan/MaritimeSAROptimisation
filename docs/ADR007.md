# ADR007: The random velocity is sized by the current at the start

**Status.** Decided by Aditya on 9 Oct 2026: a smooth rule fitted on all dev starts, and the
next benchmark carries it (`rvc`) and the pooled size (`rv`) as the comparison. The rule's
shape was then chosen by the data (§2).
**Date.** 2026-10-09.
**Amends.** ADR005 (σ_u was one pooled number; it is now a function of the start current).
T_L = 25.7 h and the model's three terms (D002) are unchanged.
**Project record.** Vault `decisions/D033 The noise is sized by the current at the start.md`.

## Question

D030's random velocity has one size, σ_u = 0.226 m/s, everywhere. It covers 90 % of the
undrogued dev buoys on average, but not by water (vault L23, L35):

- in quiet water the cloud is too wide;
- in the Gulf Stream jet it is far too narrow: the real buoy was found 0.33 of the time
  where POS promised 0.83 at 1 h.

**What should set σ_u, and how?**

## Decision

| Row | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **What sets it** | HYCOM's own current speed at the last known position, at the call | It is the one thing a forecaster has when the call comes in. Never the buoy's later speed, and never "the best of several sizes for this buoy": both use the answer, and a benchmark built on them would report a POS no real search could reach |
| **The shape** | σ_u = √(a² + (b·s)²), plus the person's crosswind slide in quadrature | Two independent errors whose variances add: a floor that does not care about the current, and one proportional to it. Fits the bins better than a straight line (§2) |
| **The numbers** | **a = 0.176 m/s** (95 % CI 0.168–0.185), **b = 0.407** (0.375–0.455), slide 0.035 m/s | `calibrate_sigma rule`, job 59372 |
| **The cap** | s ≤ **1.66 m/s**, the 95th percentile of the fastest bin's starts | The rule is never extrapolated into faster water than the buoys measured |
| **Where it lives** | `sar.model.position.SIGMA_U_RULE` and `sigma_u_for_current`; `--sigma-u auto` (the ensemble's default); `scenario_search(..., sigma_u="by-current")`; benchmark noise `rvc` | One rule, read the same way by the engine and the calibration (tested) |
| **The comparison** | `rv` (pooled 0.226) stays | As `--turn-rate inf` does for the helicopter (ADR006) |

What it gives, σ_u in m/s:

| Start current | 0 | 0.1 | 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.8 | 1.0 | 1.2 | 1.5 | ≥ 1.66 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| σ_u | 0.180 | 0.184 | 0.217 | 0.271 | 0.372 | 0.445 | 0.520 | 0.636 | 0.698 |

## 1 · The measurement

The random-velocity ladder of D030 (job 58974: 9,890 undrogued dev windows at σ_u 0.12–0.32)
was extended for the 783 windows that start in ≥ 0.5 m/s of current, at σ_u 0.36–0.9 (job
59359, `ladder --stage1 --min-start-speed 0.5`). For each bin of start current, σ_u* is where
90 % of buoys are inside the 90 % region at 4 h, with 95 % intervals from resampling whole
shared-water groups:

| Start current | Windows | Groups | σ_u* | 95 % CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–0.15 m/s | 2,448 | 95 | 0.183 | 0.175–0.189 |
| 0.15–0.3 | 3,996 | 102 | 0.196 | 0.189–0.204 |
| 0.3–0.5 | 2,663 | 95 | 0.235 | 0.226–0.245 |
| 0.5–0.75 | 636 | 71 | 0.310 | 0.288–0.336 |
| 0.75–1.0 | 103 | 34 | 0.401 | 0.364–0.454 |
| > 1.0 | 44 | 17 | 0.519 | 0.380–0.627 |

The pooled 0.226 sits in the middle: too wide for the 6,444 slow windows, a third too narrow
above 0.75 m/s.

## 2 · The fit, and why this shape

`calibrate_sigma rule` gives every window its own σ_u from a candidate rule, reads that
window's chance of being inside off its own ladder (interpolated in log σ_u between the rungs
either side), and chooses the two numbers so that every bin is covered as near 90 % as
possible: it minimises Σ groups × (coverage − 0.9)², the confirmation's own test made the
objective. Both shapes were fitted, and the one with the smaller loss is kept, a rule written
into the code before the real data were fitted.

| Shape | a | b | Loss | Coverage by bin, slow → fast |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **√(a² + (b s)²)** | **0.176** | **0.407** | **0.051** | 0.897, 0.906, 0.901, 0.888, 0.901, 0.946 |
| a + b s | 0.149 | 0.269 | 0.130 | 0.882, 0.921, 0.921, 0.900, 0.881, 0.905 |

The straight line cannot be flat in slow water and steep in fast water at once, so it
under-covers the slowest bin and over-covers the next two, where two thirds of the windows
are. The quadrature shape is within ±0.012 of 90 % in the five populated bins. It
over-covers the jet bin (0.946): that bin has 17 groups and a σ_u* interval of 0.38–0.63, and
a cloud slightly too wide there errs on the safe side.

**Checked on planted data** (`tests/validate/test_calibrate_sigma.py`). Windows built with a
known straight line are given back as that line, inside its 95 % interval. Windows built
with a known quadrature are told apart from a line (loss 0.14 against 4.2), with a inside its
interval and b about 3 % high. Reading coverage between rungs leans slightly wide, the safe
side.

## 3 · Confirmation

`ladder --sigma-u-rule`), scored at 1–24 h. The pass test, set before the run: each bin with
≥ 10 groups is inside the 90 % region 87–93 % of the time at 4 h.

**It passes.** The five populated bins are inside 87–93 % at 4 h, and the jet is at 93.2 %
(41 of 44 windows; one window is 2.3 points), on the wide side. The pooled 0.226 at 4 h gives
about 95 % in the slowest bin and **43 % above 1 m/s** (the ladder at 0.22). So the
calm-water cloud is now smaller, and the jet's is about twice as wide, 786 km² against
97 km² in calm water. Coverage stays 90.6–91.8 % pooled from 1 to 24 h, so matching at 4 h
holds across the search and beyond. `derived/sigma/rvc/confirmation.json`.

## What it leaves out

- **σ_u is fixed for the run** from the start point. A buoy that drifts from moderate water
  into the jet mid-search keeps the slower water's σ_u.
- **T_L stays 25.7 h everywhere.** A shorter memory in the jet is possible and not measured
  (ADR005's open item).
- **The jet is thin.** 44 windows in 17 groups above 1 m/s; the cap stops the rule beyond
  1.66 m/s.
