## Question

The paper compares a learned searcher against **what the Coast Guard actually does**. That baseline
has three parts: the numbers (sweep width, speeds, range, readiness), how detection works inside the
sweep, and the geometry of the patterns. Until now:

- the sweep width, 185 m, had **no citation since 3 Sep**;
- the search speed was **recorded nowhere**;
- #48 specified the Expanding Square as **fixed to the ground**.

What does the baseline fly, at what numbers, and how does it detect?

## Source

One document, cited throughout by table and printed page: **U.S. Coast Guard, *U.S. Coast Guard
Addendum to the United States National Search and Rescue Supplement (NSS) to the International
Aeronautical and Maritime Search and Rescue Manual (IAMSAR)*, COMDTINST M16130.2F, 7 January 2013**
("the Addendum"). It is the manual Coast Guard search planners work from, which is the reason to
prefer it over secondary sources. Every figure below was read off the page, not off a summary.

## Evidence

### 1 · The code reproduces the manual's own worked example

p. 3-23 (d): "if the search speed were 90 knots and the sweep width was 0.1 NM then a single
six-sector pattern with a radius of 1.5 NM (distance covered in one minute at 90 knots) would
achieve a coverage of about 0.19 in about 9 minutes." `sector_search()` at those inputs flies
**9 legs, 25,002 m (= 9R), in 540 s**. The same rule is on p. 3-28 a.: "Trackline = 9 x R NM".

One window of Expanding Square at W and 90 kt is **51 legs, 125,010 m (67.5 NM), a square about
4.6 km across**, sweeping about 23 km² if overlap is ignored.

### 2 · Why waypoints and not 60 s samples

At S = 185.2 m and 46.3 m/s, the first leg takes **4 s**. Legs outlast one 60 s step only after
about **16 minutes**, when the leg count passes 30. So if you sample the path every 60 s and join
the samples, the path cuts straight across the first third of the pattern. It would sweep water
the helicopter never flew over and miss water it did. **The sweep (#45) and the episode runner
(#47) must follow the waypoints**, all of the sub-legs inside a step, not the chord between two
step positions.

### 3 · A moving target outruns the step

A target in the Gulf Stream at 1.8 m/s moves **108 m in one 60 s step, more than W/2 = 92.6 m**.
Sweeping against the particles' positions at the start of the step, as #47 proposes, can therefore
miss a target the helicopter passed right over, or count one it never reached. Detection should use
the **closest point of approach** between the helicopter's motion and each particle's motion over
the step. The site does this for its single target.

### 4 · What a marker-following pattern does and does not correct

- **It corrects the current.** Under a uniform current, the ground path is the fixed pattern
  shifted by current × t (`tests/search/test_patterns.py`).
- **It does not correct the leeway.** The marker does not feel the wind, but a person does. At a
  10 m/s wind and 2 % leeway (D002), the person moves **0.2 m/s relative to the marker: 540 m in
  45 minutes, about six times W/2** (`tests/search/test_datum.py`).

That gap is not a modelling shortcut. It is a real weakness of the doctrinal method, and it is
exactly the kind of information a searcher that sees the drift field could use.

### 5 · The four patterns against one real buoy (26 Sep, site, real data)

Buoy 300234062329360 (sealed: watched, not tuned on), reported 2019-01-01 00:00 UTC; base 194 NM
out; datum drifted with the current only; on scene about 2 h 03 min after the call; **datum error
1.11 km**. Same set-up for all four.

| Pattern | First leg | Found, into the pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Sector Search | 42°, the drift at the datum | 8 min 33 s |
| Expanding Square | 42° | 9 min 58 s |
| Parallel Track | 37°, the datum line | 22 min 19 s |
| Trackline Return | 37°, the datum line | **22 s** |

The Trackline finds it at once because this buoy did drift along the predicted path, just further
than predicted, and the Trackline is the one pattern that searches that line first. **One buoy is
an illustration, not a result.** Which pattern wins on average is the comparison the paper makes
(#47), not something this table shows.

## Consequences

- **#48 changes wording**: the pattern is flown relative to the drifting marker, not fixed to the
  ground. **D004's baseline changes with it.** The common-datum protocol (the same datum for every
  strategy) is unchanged.
- **#47 needs the marker's track** for scripted patterns (`sar.search.datum.marker_track`), and must
  sweep sub-legs (§2) with closest-approach detection (§3).
- **`frontend/src/geo.js` `SWEEP_WIDTH_M` is 185.2, not 185.** It is a 0.1 % change, and it now
  traces to a table.
- **Limitations, recorded in the vault's register:**
  - W is a calm-weather value. It halves in winds above 15 kt (Table H-10: 0.5) and quarters above
    25 kt, and **no scenario correction is applied**.
  - A lifejacket would multiply W by four (p. H-41 (d)).
  - **Turns are instantaneous.** A standard-rate turn (3° per second) at 90 kt has a radius of
    about 880 m, which is more than the Expanding Square's first several legs.
- **Two metres-per-degree constants coexist.** `sar.utils.geo.M_PER_DEG_LAT` is 111,320 m;
  `sar.model.position` uses R·π/180 = 111,195 m. That is 0.11 % apart. The patterns use the
  former, and the drift of the marker uses the latter, through `DriftPipeline`. It is below every
  other error here, but it is stated so nobody rediscovers it.

- **Four patterns, not two.** PS and TSR are in `sar.search.patterns` and on the site (#75).
  Whether the paper's baseline stays the Expanding Square (D004), or compares all four, is the
  search half's call; nothing here changes D004.

## Not decided here

- **Whether W varies by scenario** with the weather correction. If it does, every strategy in a
  scenario must get the same W.
- **Two or more helicopters** (VM, multi-unit parallel track).
- **Verification against IAMSAR Vol. II Appendix N**, which the Addendum points to ("Table N-7
  provides corresponding information", p. H-41) but which could not be opened. The same goes for
  the newer Addendum revision, M16130.2H.
