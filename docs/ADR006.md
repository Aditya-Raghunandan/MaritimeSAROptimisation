# ADR006: The helicopter cannot turn on the spot

**Status.** Decided by Aditya on 9 Oct 2026. Suné's PPO agent issues turn commands under it,
so its interface (§ "For the trained agent") needs her agreement before her next training run.
**Date.** 2026-10-09.
**Amends.** ADR003 (the patterns are flown by an autopilot, not along their square corners)
and ADR004 (a step is still a 60 s short path, but one the helicopter could have flown).
**Project record.** Vault `decisions/D032 The helicopter turns at 7 degrees a second.md`
holds the framing and the citations. This is the engineering contract.

## Question

Until now every searcher turned instantly. The Expanding Square was flown with square
corners, the Parallel Track reversed onto a track 185 m away at once, and a heading searcher
could swing 180° between two minutes. Aditya found it flying the open-day game by hand.

A helicopter at 90 kt turns the way an aeroplane does: it banks, and the tilted lift curves
its path. Measured on synthetic clouds on 9 Oct, greedy turned a median 122° at every
decision. A real helicopter turning that much would have spent most of the window turning.

**How fast can the helicopter turn, how does each searcher fly with that limit, and how does
the referee make sure nobody turns faster?**

## 1 · Why 7 degrees a second

In a level, balanced turn the lift is tilted by the bank φ. Its horizontal part, m g tan φ,
is the centripetal force m V ω, so

$$\omega = \frac{g \tan\varphi}{V}, \qquad r = \frac{V}{\omega} = \frac{V^2}{g\tan\varphi}.$$

At V = 90 kt = 46.3 m/s:

| Turn | Bank | ω | r | 90° | 180° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard rate, the instrument-flight norm (FAA Helicopter Flying Handbook ch. 12: 360° in 2 min) | 13.9° | 3.0 deg/s | 884 m | 30 s | 60 s |
| **30° bank (this decision)** | **30°** | **7.0 deg/s** | **379 m** | **12.8 s** | **25.7 s** |

**Why 30°.** It is the most the FAA's procedures use for a routine turn. AIM ch. 5 §3,
Holding, "Bank Angle": "make all turns ... at: 3 degrees per second, or 30 degree bank angle,
or 25 degree bank angle, provided a flight director system is used". It is also what autopilot
flight directors hold a turn to (e.g. Garmin's roll mode "limits bank angle to 30°"). A crew
searching by eye in daylight over open sea flies by sight, not on instruments, so the gentler
standard rate is a lower bound, not the norm. Nothing published gives an MH-60T's bank in a
search, so 30° is the procedural cap and is stated as such.

**What it is set against.** The Expanding Square's first leg is 185 m (4 s). The Parallel
Track's tracks are 185 m apart, against a turn diameter of 757 m, so every reversal is a
loop. IAMSAR recognises the same constraint. Vol. II (2019) §5.5.5 says the Expanding Square
is "often appropriate for vessels, small boats or helicopters, but not necessarily
fixed-wing aircraft". §5.5.11 says the Creeping Line "requires many more turns ... [so] it is
usually not as efficient".

## 2 · The autopilot

A pattern is waypoints with square corners. Something must decide how a helicopter that
turns at most ω flies them. The answer is the L1 guidance law (Park, Deyst & How, AIAA GNC
2004, paper 2004-4900), the path follower flown by ArduPilot and PX4. Each 0.5 s tick it does
this (`sar.search.kinematics.follow`):

1. **Where it is along the path.** The nearest point of the drawn path, searched only a
   little ahead of the last one, so a loop cannot jump it backwards.
2. **The reference point.** Where the drawn path leaves a circle of radius L1 about the
   helicopter. If the path is farther than L1 away, the nearest point instead.
3. **The angle off the nose**, η, from the heading to the reference point.
4. **The turn.** a = 2V² sin η / L1, a rate of 2V sin η / L1, never more than ω. A point
   behind (|η| > 90°) gets the full rate toward it.
5. **The tick's arc**, flown exactly.

Nothing is planned ahead. The path comes from correcting, every half second, toward a point
that moves along the drawing. A corner the helicopter can round, it rounds. Where legs are
closer than the turn diameter, the reference point swings behind it and the helicopter loops
round to the next leg, which is the loop ("bulb") turn agricultural coverage planning uses
when tracks are closer than the turn diameter (Bochtis & Vougioukas 2008).

**L1 = r.** Two reasons. First, a 90° fly-by turn of radius r starts r·tan 45° = r before the
corner, and that is where the reference point starts round it. Second, it keeps the flown path
closest to the drawing. The mean distance from the flown path to the drawn legs over the 45
minutes (9 Oct):

| L1 / r | 0.5 | 0.75 | **1.0** | 1.25 | 1.5 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expanding Square | 14.0 m | 20.8 | **17.5** | 23.7 | 30.7 | 43.3 |
| Parallel Track | 34.2 | 25.3 | **21.5** | 25.3 | 32.2 | 51.4 |
| Sector | 56.8 | 44.9 | **38.3** | 35.4 | 34.8 | 42.9 |
| Trackline Return | 22.9 | 17.2 | **13.9** | 17.0 | 20.7 | 28.9 |
| Sum | 127.9 | 108.2 | **91.2** | 101.4 | 118.4 | 166.5 |

Patterns that go on for ever are drawn for two windows (`DRAWN_WINDOWS`), because the
autopilot rounds corners and gets along the drawing faster than the clock. The Parallel
Track's area is the window's, so it is drawn once. With loops it never reaches the end.

## 3 · The referee's check

Every waypoint carries the heading at that moment (`Waypoints.heading_deg`). A chord dt long,
turning a = |Δh| between its ends, is accepted only if a helicopter at speed V, turning one
way at no more than ω, could fly it (`sar.search.kinematics.check_turns`):

1. **Rate.** a ≤ ω dt, and a ≤ 30° per chord so its direction is unambiguous.
2. **Speed.** With the turn made at full rate in τ = a/ω and the rest straight, the chord's
   length lies between (straight time split either side of the turn)
   V(dt − τ) cos(a/2) + 2(V/ω) sin(a/2) and (all straight time on the bisector)
   V(dt − τ) + 2(V/ω) sin(a/2).
3. **Direction.** It points off the start heading by between a·τ/(2dt) and a(1 − τ/(2dt)),
   the average heading of the earliest and the latest the turn can be made. A slack of a³/5
   radians allows for the difference between an average heading and a chord's direction.

An arc at constant rate meets all three exactly. A square corner fails the first, a corner
disguised by slowing down fails the second, and a turn made at once and hidden in a long
chord fails the third. The browser's referee (`frontend/src/kinematics.js`) runs the same
check and is held to Python by `fixtures/search_golden.json` (turns, ten minutes of each
pattern flown tick by tick) and `fixtures/referee_golden.json` (every searcher, and a player).

## For the trained agent

- **The action is a turn command**: degrees, right positive, flown at ω for up to the step,
  then straight (`episode.turn(deg)` or a policy returning `Turn(deg)`). A heading command
  (`episode.step(heading)`) is still accepted.
- **The heading is part of the state** (`episode.heading_deg`). With turns limited, the same
  map and position with a different heading have different best moves, so an agent that
  cannot see its heading is solving a problem it cannot observe.
- `recorded()` stores the flown path with headings, so a trained agent's flights replay in
  the site the same way the benchmark's do.

## Measured: what the instant turn was worth

Synthetic steady clouds, 10⁴ particles, current (1.0, 0.3) m/s, 5 seeds, the benchmark's own
searchers. POS at 45 min, instant → 7.0 deg/s (9 Oct):

| Searcher | 1.5 km cloud | change | 3 km cloud | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expanding Square | 0.793 → 0.749 | −4.4 pts | 0.331 → 0.320 | −1.1 |
| Parallel Track | 0.786 → 0.686 | **−10.0** | 0.326 → 0.273 | −5.3 |
| Trackline Return | 0.719 → 0.669 | −5.0 | 0.291 → 0.266 | −2.5 |
| Sector | 0.325 → 0.367 | +4.2 | 0.146 → 0.171 | +2.5 |
| Greedy (36/60 s) | 0.614 → 0.611 | −0.3 | 0.276 → 0.275 | −0.1 |
| Random | 0.053 → 0.055 | +0.2 | 0.036 → 0.038 | +0.1 |

The instant turn flattered the Parallel Track about twice as much as the Expanding Square.
So the dev benchmark's "Parallel beats the Square in 107 of 107 groups" (D031), measured with
instant turns, must be measured again. The Sector's gain is not yet explained; its drawn
pattern re-flies its own centre on every leg, and rounding the corners spreads it out.

## What it leaves out

- **Roll-in.** The bank is reached at once. Rolling into 30° takes a second or two, which
  moves where a turn starts by about 50–100 m along the track.
- **Slowing for a tight turn.** The speed is constant (90 kt, where the sweep widths are
  stated). A real crew would slow down to fly the Expanding Square's first legs, as IAMSAR's
  note suggests.
- **Wind on the turn.** The turn is a circle relative to the marker. In wind, a turn at
  constant bank is a circle in the air, so over the water it is stretched downwind.
- **The arrival.** Everyone arrives on the drift heading. A real transit arrives on its
  bearing from base, and the Search view does use that.
